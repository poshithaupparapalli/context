On Reading Slowly

Most of what we call reading today is skimming with better manners. We move across paragraphs the way a thumb moves across glass, collecting the gist and discarding the grain of the sentences that carried it.

The long book

When I was nineteen I carried The Brothers Karamazov across three countries and finished it only on the way home. What stayed with me was not the plot but the argument underneath it: whether a person can hold faith and honest doubt in the same mind without one corrupting the other.

That tension is the quiet engine of any serious essay. The writer who knows gradient descent from the inside understands that you do not leap to the answer; you take small steps downhill and trust the slope. Reading slowly is the same bet, placed on the page instead of on a loss function.
